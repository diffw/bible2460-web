Privacy Policy

Effective date: October 20, 2026

Bible 2460 ("the App") is developed and operated by Bible 2460 ("we", "us" or "our"). This Privacy Policy explains what information the App handles, which service providers receive it, what happens when you share a verse, and the choices you have. It also covers our website, bible2460.com.

In short

  • You don't need an account. Your profile (including your denomination), your favorites, the verses you read and your reminder times stay on your device.
  • The App sends limited usage, device and purchase information to three service providers: Mixpanel (product analytics), RevenueCat (in-app purchases) and Meta (measuring our ads on Facebook and Instagram). Our website uses Google Analytics.
  • The App shows no ads. It never asks for permission to track you, and it does not collect Apple's advertising identifier (IDFA).
  • A verse is shared only when you tap to share, and only with the app or person you choose.
  • Reminders are local notifications created on your device. The App can add images to your photo library but cannot see your library.
  • There is no in-app switch to turn off analytics or ad measurement. Deleting the App stops it (Section 15).

1. What the App collects

You don't need to create an account, and the App never asks for your email address or phone number. We do not run our own servers for the App. Through the three service providers described in Sections 3 to 5, the App collects:

  • Random IDs created for the App on your device (they are not your name or Apple ID), and Apple's identifier for vendor, an ID that iOS gives to apps from the same developer (sent to RevenueCat).
  • How you use the App, such as when it is installed and opened and how long it stays open, how far you get in the first-time setup (not your answers), your choice on the setup's reminder step, use of the verse screen (not which verse), when the subscription screen is shown, purchases you start and how they end, and whether you use a Home Screen widget.
  • Purchase information from the App Store, such as the product, price, currency, dates and App Store transaction numbers (sent to RevenueCat and Meta).
  • Device and app information, such as device model, iOS version, language, region and time zone settings, App Store country, mobile carrier, screen size and app version.
  • IP address and approximate location. Our service providers see your IP address when the App connects to them. Mixpanel uses it to estimate your country, region and city, and Meta may use it to estimate your approximate location.
  • Crash and error reports about the Meta SDK's own code, if Meta turns these features on.

We use this information to provide and restore your purchases, to understand how the App is used and improve it, and to measure and improve our ads. It does not include your name or contact details, but some of it can be connected to your device or your purchases over time, so we treat it as information about you. Each provider handles information under its own terms and privacy policy, and may process it in the United States and other countries.

Because Bible 2460 is a Bible app, simply using it may suggest an interest in the Christian faith.

2. What stays on your device

Your profile answers (your name, gender and denomination, if you give them), your favorite verses, the verses, topics and themes you read or pick, and your reminder times stay on your device, except for what you choose to share (Section 9). We do not send them to Mixpanel, RevenueCat or Meta (for Meta, see also Section 5), and we never see them. Mixpanel only receives your choice on the setup's reminder step and whether iOS allowed notifications (Section 3). The App never reads your contacts, your photo library or your precise location.

The App keeps these on your device:

  • Your settings, such as interface language, Bible translation, topics, themes and display options.
  • Your profile, if you fill it in, and your favorite verses.
  • Your daily verse reminder settings, the reminders scheduled on your device (up to 30 days ahead), and the free-trial reminder if you turn it on (Section 10).
  • Setup and tip progress, and the share options you used most recently, so the App can show them first.
  • What the Home Screen widgets need: your settings, theme files and a copy of your Pro status. The widgets do not connect to the internet and cannot read your profile or favorites.
  • Technical data for our service providers: their random IDs, a short queue of analytics events waiting to be sent (at most 24, dropped after 4 days), a record of the days the App was opened so each day is counted once, purchase information cached by RevenueCat, and events the Meta SDK has not yet sent, a record of the purchases it has already reported, its session timing and its cached settings.

Backups. Your profile, your favorites and the App's Mixpanel data are left out of device backups, and the App does not sync anything through iCloud. This means your profile and favorites do not move to a new iPhone or come back when you restore a backup. Your other settings, reminder settings, setup progress, recent share options, widget data, and the IDs and other data stored by the RevenueCat and Meta SDKs may be included in your own iCloud or computer backups, depending on your device settings, and can come back when you restore one.

Deleting the App removes what it stores on your device. Images you saved to your photo library and text you copied stay until you remove them.

3. Mixpanel (product analytics)

We use Mixpanel to understand how the App is used, for example how many people come back, where people stop during setup, and which parts of the App lead to purchases.

Mixpanel receives a random analytics ID created on your device (it is not included in backups and is not connected to your profile, your favorites or the IDs used by RevenueCat or Meta), and these app events:

  • the App being opened (counted at most once a day);
  • each first-time setup step you reach (never your answers), and on the reminder step, whether you chose to turn reminders on or "Not now" and whether iOS allowed notifications;
  • meaningful use of the verse screen, such as staying on it, browsing or saving a favorite (but not which verse);
  • the subscription screen being shown, and which part of the App opened it;
  • purchases started in the App: the plan (annual, monthly or Lifetime), which screen it started from, whether it succeeded, is pending, was cancelled or failed, whether it was a free trial or a paid purchase, and whether Pro was unlocked;
  • opening the App from a widget, and whether a Home Screen widget is set up.

Each event also includes the time, the app version, your device model and iOS version, whether you have Pro, and random IDs that group events from the same session and, where relevant, from the same setup or purchase. Mixpanel does not receive App Store transaction numbers, receipts, product IDs, prices, currency, your App Store country, your reminder times, or anything about what you share. We switch off other device details that Mixpanel would normally collect, such as screen size, network type and mobile carrier.

Events go to Mixpanel's servers in the United States. Mixpanel uses the IP address of each request to estimate your country, region and city, and then does not store the IP address. Mixpanel deletes events automatically two years after they are collected. Mixpanel's privacy policy

4. RevenueCat (in-app purchases)

Subscriptions and the Lifetime purchase are sold through Apple's App Store. We use RevenueCat to check your App Store purchases, unlock and restore Pro, see whether you can get a free trial, and view subscription statistics.

RevenueCat receives:

  • a random ID its SDK creates on your device, and Apple's identifier for vendor;
  • your App Store purchase information, such as products, prices, currency, purchase and expiry dates, transaction numbers and receipts;
  • device and app information, such as device model, iOS version, your preferred languages (including the language you chose for the App, so the subscription screen matches it), App Store country, app version and your device's App Tracking Transparency status;
  • a record each time the App shows its subscription screen, including which offers were shown; and
  • your IP address. If a subscription screen design is set up in RevenueCat, the SDK may also download its images and fonts from RevenueCat in advance.

We do not give RevenueCat your name, your email address or the IDs used by Mixpanel or Meta, and the App sends RevenueCat no ad-attribution data. RevenueCat's privacy policy

5. Meta (advertising measurement)

We advertise Bible 2460 on Meta's platforms, such as Facebook and Instagram. The App includes the Meta (Facebook) SDK so that we can measure how well these ads work and so that Meta can show them to people who are likely to be interested. The SDK starts when the App opens. The App itself shows no ads.

The SDK automatically sends Meta:

  • A random ID that it creates on your device. It stays the same until you delete the App, and it can come back if you restore a device backup.
  • App events: the install, each time the App is opened or closed and for how long, and technical events about the SDK itself.
  • In-app purchase events, such as starting a checkout, starting a free trial or a subscription, a free trial turning into a paid subscription, a Lifetime purchase, purchases already active on your Apple account when the App first starts, and some failed purchases. These include the product and its name and description, the price and currency, purchase details such as the date and subscription period, and App Store transaction numbers (on some purchase paths, the App Store receipt instead). These numbers stay the same for the same purchase across reinstalls and devices. Depending on settings that Meta controls, the SDK may also log other purchase activity, such as renewals.
  • Device and app information, such as device model, iOS version, language, region and time zone settings, mobile carrier, screen size, number of processor cores, app version and build, and technical details of each event, such as its time and the technical name of the App screen that was open.
  • Crash and error reports about the SDK's own code, only if Meta turns these features on.

Meta also receives your IP address and may use it to estimate your approximate location. We have not limited how Meta may use these events, so Meta may use them both to report on our ads and to improve how they are delivered. Meta's Business Tools Terms also allow Meta to use event data to improve its products and to personalize the ads and content it shows people, within the limits Meta applies to events sent without tracking permission (Section 7).

The App adds no events, user IDs or contact details of its own. We do not send Meta your name, your profile answers, the IDs used by Mixpanel or RevenueCat, or the verses, topics, themes or favorites you view or save. Meta's automatic advanced matching and event suggestions are turned off in our Meta settings, and we have not set up codeless events, so the Meta SDK does not collect what you type or the buttons you tap in the App. Sharing a verse to Instagram or Facebook does not go through the Meta SDK (Section 9). Meta's privacy policy

6. Our website and earlier versions of the App

Our website. Our website, bible2460.com, uses Google Analytics to understand how people use the site. Google Analytics uses cookies and receives information such as your IP address, your browser and device type, and the pages you visit. Google may use your IP address to estimate your approximate location. Our pages also load fonts from Google Fonts, so Google sees your IP address when a page loads, and the company that hosts our website sees your IP address and standard request details. Google's privacy policy

Earlier versions of the App. Starting with version 1.6.0, the App uses only the verse text that comes with it. Earlier versions could ask bible-api.com for a verse that was not included in the App. Such a request contains the verse reference and translation (for example, "John 3:16" and "kjv"), the basic technical details any app sends with a web request (such as the app and iOS version and your preferred language) and your IP address, but no device identifiers.

7. Ads and tracking

  • The App does not show ads.
  • We advertise the App on Meta's platforms and measure those ads with the Meta SDK (Section 5).
  • The App never asks for permission to track you through Apple's App Tracking Transparency, and we have turned off the Meta SDK's collection of Apple's advertising identifier (IDFA). As a result, every event the App sends to Meta is marked as sent without tracking permission, and iOS blocks the server address that Meta uses only for tracking. Meta states that it may restrict how it uses events sent without this permission.
  • Apple's SKAdNetwork may tell Meta that an install came from one of our ads, together with a rough value that the Meta SDK sets from activity in the App, such as a purchase, without identifying you or your device.
  • We do not try to identify your device through fingerprinting, and we do not give information to data brokers.

8. Purchases and Apple

The App is distributed through Apple's App Store, and Apple processes all payments. Apple may collect data related to your download or purchase under Apple's Privacy Policy. We and our service providers never receive your payment card or bank details, or your Apple ID password.

When you buy or restore a subscription or the Lifetime purchase, your App Store purchase information goes to RevenueCat so it can check the purchase and unlock Pro (Section 4), and the Meta SDK sends purchase events, including App Store transaction numbers, to Meta (Section 5). For purchases started in the App, Mixpanel learns the plan, which screen the purchase started from, the result, whether it was a free trial and whether Pro was unlocked, as well as whether you have Pro, but never transaction numbers or prices (Section 3).

The App also reads your App Store country on your device to choose a default translation and to show the right purchase options. You can manage or cancel your subscription in your Apple account settings, which the App can open for you.

Depending on your settings, other Apple services handle information under Apple's own terms, such as iCloud or computer backups (Section 2), iCloud Photos for images you save, Universal Clipboard for things you copy, and Messages when you share a verse that way (Section 9).

9. Sharing verses

You can turn a verse into an image or a short video and share it. The App creates the image or video on your device. Nothing is shared until you tap a share option, and it goes only to the app or person you choose. Apart from handing the content to that app, the App does not report what you share or where you share it to us, Mixpanel, RevenueCat or the Meta SDK.

  • Save image saves the image or video to your photo library (Section 10).
  • Copy Text copies the verse, its reference and translation, and "-Bible 2460" to the clipboard.
  • Instagram and Facebook Stories and Reels: the App puts the image or video on the clipboard for five minutes and opens the Instagram or Facebook app. It also passes the App's own Meta app ID, which identifies the App, not you, so that Instagram or Facebook can show which app the content came from. Whether to post is up to you, and Meta handles what you post under its own terms.
  • Instagram posts and WhatsApp: the App hands a temporary copy of the file to that app and deletes the copy afterwards.
  • Messages: the App opens Apple's Messages screen with the image or video, the verse text and a link to the App on the App Store. You choose the recipients in Apple's screen. The App cannot see them or your contacts, and nothing is sent until you tap Send.
  • Share via… and other share buttons open Apple's share sheet with the file, the text or a link to the App on the App Store, and only what you choose is sent to the app or person you choose.
  • TikTok: sharing to TikTok is not available yet; when it is, the App will first save the image or video to your photo library and then open the TikTok app with that item.

If you use Universal Clipboard, Apple may make what the App puts on the clipboard available on your other Apple devices while it is there. To decide which share buttons to show, the App checks on your device which of these apps are installed. The result is not stored or sent anywhere. The App remembers only which share options you used recently (Section 2).

10. Notifications and photos

  • Notifications are optional. The App asks for permission to send notifications only when you tap to turn on daily verse reminders (during setup or in Settings) or the free-trial reminder on the subscription screen. If you tap "Not now", the App does not ask. Daily verse reminders are off until you turn them on.
  • Local notifications only. Reminders are scheduled by the App on your device and delivered by iOS. There are no push notifications and no notification server. The verse in each daily reminder is prepared on your device, up to 30 days ahead. How reminders appear on your Lock Screen depends on your iOS notification settings. The App does not record whether a reminder was delivered or opened. The only reminder-related information that leaves your device is your choice on the setup's reminder step and whether iOS allowed notifications (Section 3).
  • Free-trial reminder. The "Remind me" switch on the subscription screen is off each time the screen opens. If you turn it on, allow notifications and start an Annual free trial, the App schedules one local notification before the trial ends (about two days before a 7-day trial ends, or one day before a 3-day trial ends), between 9 a.m. and 8 p.m. local time. The reminder exists only on that device. It does not cancel or change your subscription, and if you cancel the trial elsewhere it may still appear.
  • Photos: add only. The App asks for "add only" access to your photo library the first time you tap Save image. It can add the images and videos you create, but it cannot see, read or change anything else in your library.
  • Not requested. The App does not ask for tracking permission, and it does not ask for access to your location, camera, microphone, contacts, calendars, Bluetooth, local network or health data.

You can change notification and photo access at any time in iOS Settings.

11. Feedback and reviews

  • Send feedback opens your email app with a draft addressed to hi@sfa.vision. The draft includes your device model, iOS version, and the App's version and build number, to help us fix problems. Nothing is sent unless you send the email yourself, and you can edit or delete these details first. If you email us, we receive your email address and whatever you write, and we use it to reply to you and to improve the App.
  • Leave a review opens the App Store. Ratings and reviews are handled by Apple.

12. How long information is kept

  • On your device: until you delete it or delete the App. Content placed on the clipboard for Stories and Reels expires after five minutes, temporary share files are deleted after use, and scheduled reminders stay with iOS until they are delivered, you turn reminders off, or you delete the App. Images and videos you save stay in your photo library until you delete them.
  • Mixpanel: events are deleted automatically two years after they are collected.
  • RevenueCat: purchase records are kept for as long as we use RevenueCat to support your purchases, and as needed for accounting and legal reasons.
  • Meta and Google: according to their own policies and settings.
  • Emails you send us: for as long as we need them to handle your request.

13. Children's privacy

The App does not knowingly collect personal information from children under the age of 13 (or the age set by the law where you live). The information described in Sections 3 to 5 is collected automatically from everyone who uses the App, without knowing their age. If you believe a child has given us personal information, please contact us.

14. Security

Information on your device is protected by the security features built into iOS. The App sends information to its service providers over encrypted (HTTPS) connections, and these providers are responsible for protecting the information they receive. No way of sending or storing information is completely secure.

15. Your choices and rights

  • No in-app switch. The App does not have a setting to turn off analytics or ad measurement. To stop all of the collection described in Sections 3 to 5, delete the App.
  • Reminders and permissions. You can turn daily reminders off in the App's Settings, and change notification or photo access in iOS Settings.
  • Deleting the App removes what it stores on your device, including its random IDs. If you install the App again, new random IDs are created. Some things can still connect an old and a new install: Apple's identifier for vendor changes only after you delete all of our apps from your device, App Store transaction numbers stay the same for the same purchase, and the IDs created by the Meta and RevenueCat SDKs can come back if you restore a backup made while the App was installed. Deleting the App does not remove images you saved to your photo library.
  • Meta's own controls. If you use Facebook or Instagram, you can review and disconnect activity that businesses share with Meta in your Meta account settings, under "Your activity off Meta technologies".
  • Requests. Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct or delete your personal information, or to object to how it is used. Email us at hi@sfa.vision. The App does not show you its random IDs, and our service providers know you by those IDs and, for purchases, by App Store transaction numbers, so we usually cannot find your records from your name or email address. We will tell you what information we need and what we can do, and we will respond as required by applicable law. You can also contact Mixpanel, RevenueCat, Meta or Google directly about the information they hold.
